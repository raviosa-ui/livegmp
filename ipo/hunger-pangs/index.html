About Hunger Pangs

Hunger Pangs Limited owns and operates 'Burma Burma Restaurant & Tea Room', India's largest specialty Burmese casual dining restaurant chain. Founded in 2013 and operational since 2014, the company offers an experiential Pan-Asian casual dining concept rooted in Burmese culinary culture, featuring an all-vegetarian and alcohol-free menu. As of July 31, 2026, the company operates 22 restaurants across nine cities spanning North, South, East, and West India, along with one delivery kitchen in Mumbai. Its scale is supported by a dedicated research and development lab in Mumbai and three regional warehouses in Bhiwandi, Begur, and Okhla.

Business Model

The company operates in a single segment—restaurant and food service—utilizing a fully company-owned and company-operated model. Revenue is predominantly derived from dine-in casual dining services, which contributed 88.82% of revenue from operations in Fiscal 2026. Delivery sales via platforms like Swiggy and Zomato accounted for 10.76% of Fiscal 2026 revenue, complemented by direct-to-consumer sales of packaged teas and snacks via the 'Burma Burma Shop' platform. The company serves individual retail consumers, reporting an average bill value of ₹2,400 in Fiscal 2026.

Objects of the Issue

  • Expenditure towards setting up 27 New Burma Burma Restaurants across India aggregating ₹1,172.92 million.
  • Funding general corporate purposes, subject to not exceeding 25% of the gross proceeds of the fresh issue.

Promoters

The promoters are Ankit Virendra Gupta and Chirag Narendra Chhajer, serving as Joint Managing Directors. They hold 33.35% each (aggregating 66.70%) of pre-offer equity capital. Gupta has over 17 years of experience in food service and operations, while Chhajer possesses over 12 years of experience managing business operations and finance.

Strengths

  • Largest specialty Burmese restaurant chain in India with a distinct cultural identity and dining concept.
  • Demonstrated multi-city scalability with a national network of 22 restaurants across nine cities.
  • Company-owned and company-operated model supported by standardized processes and technological controls.
  • Consistently high brand affinity and guest ratings, holding a 4.71 Zomato rating in Fiscal 2026.
  • Experienced promoter-led management team with extensive domain expertise in hospitality.

Key Risks

  • History of restated net losses, reporting losses of ₹56.87 million, ₹5.05 million, and ₹153.72 million in Fiscals 2024, 2025, and 2026.
  • All-vegetarian and no-alcohol format may restrict the overall addressable customer base and customer footfall.
  • Capital deployment of ₹1,172.92 million for restaurant expansion is based entirely on internal management estimates without independent appraisal.
  • Execution risks concerning opening 27 planned restaurants, which may face delays, cost overruns, or slower store profitability.
  • Dependence on continuous access to specialty Burmese ingredients and retention of skilled chefs and managers.

Points to Weigh

  • Demonstrated robust top-line momentum, with revenue from operations growing at a 47.05% CAGR from FY24 to FY26.
  • Operational profitability at the EBITDA level is countered by persistent net losses, with net loss widening significantly in FY26.
  • Significant growth plans funded by issue proceeds to set up 27 new outlets, intensifying future operational execution demands.
  • Distinctive vegetarian Burmese focus provides clear differentiation in Pan-Asian dining but naturally constrains the broader addressable market.
