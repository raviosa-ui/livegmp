About South Eastern Coalfields

South Eastern Coalfields Limited, incorporated in 1985, is one of the largest coal producers in India in terms of production. In Fiscal 2026, the company produced 176.29 million tonnes of coal, accounting for 16.94% of India's total coal production. Operating from its registered office in Bilaspur, Chhattisgarh, the company had 53 producing mines as of September 30, 2026, comprising 19 opencast mines and 34 underground mines located across the Central India, Korba, and Mand-Raigarh coalfields.

Business Model

The company primarily earns its revenue from the sale of raw (unwashed) non-coking coal across gross calorific value grades from G3 to G16. Its primary customers belong to the power sector, including central and state power generating companies, independent power producers, and captive power plants. It also serves non-power industries such as aluminium, cement, sponge iron, and fertilizer sectors.

Promoters

The promoters of the company are the President of India, acting through the Ministry of Coal, Government of India, and Coal India Limited. Coal India Limited holds 100% of the pre-offer paid-up equity share capital.

Strengths

  • One of the largest coal producers in India operating major large-scale coal mines with a vast resource base.
  • Strategic support and vast resources from Coal India Limited.
  • Highly mechanised and technology-led mining operations driving scale and efficiency.
  • Expanding rail-led evacuation network and first-mile connectivity infrastructure.
  • Strong financial performance with healthy cash generation and a net cash balance sheet.
  • Long-standing, FSA-backed relationships with marquee power sector customers.

Key Risks

  • Information relating to resources and reserves is based on estimates and has not been audited by DMT according to international standards.
  • Revenues are primarily derived from the sale of raw non-coking coal, making the business vulnerable to demand reductions.
  • Substantial sales dependence on the power sector, exposing the company to adverse developments in that industry.
  • Business and operations are significantly influenced by Coal India Limited, and executive employees are on secondment from CIL.
  • Reliance on top customers and third-party contractual vendors for a significant portion of operations.
  • Inherent operating and safety hazards in mining operations subject to extensive government regulations.

Points to Weigh

  • The entire IPO is an offer for sale by Coal India Limited, meaning the company will not receive any proceeds from the public issue.
  • Top five customers accounted for over 35% of revenue across recent periods, showing customer concentration.
  • The company experienced declines in production, dispatch, revenue, and profit in Fiscal 2025 compared to Fiscal 2024.
  • Operations are subject to material civil, criminal, and tax litigations involving the company and its promoters.
